
TOPIC: PROGRESSIVE OVERLOAD

Progressive overload is a training principle where the
demands placed on the body are gradually increased over time.

The purpose is to provide an appropriate training stimulus
that encourages adaptation.

Progression can be achieved through:
- Increasing resistance or weight.
- Increasing repetitions.
- Increasing sets.
- Improving exercise technique.
- Increasing range of motion where appropriate.

Progression should be gradual and based on the individual's
training experience, recovery and ability.

TOPIC: STRENGTH TRAINING

Strength training involves exercises that challenge muscles
against resistance.

Common types include:
- Free weight exercises.
- Machine exercises.
- Resistance band exercises.
- Bodyweight exercises.

A training programme should consider exercise selection,
training frequency, volume, intensity and recovery.

TOPIC: MUSCLE RECOVERY

Recovery is an important part of a training programme.

Factors that influence recovery include:
- Adequate sleep.
- Appropriate nutrition.
- Managing training intensity and volume.
- Rest between demanding training sessions.
- Managing overall stress.

Training progress should be considered over time rather
than judged from a single workout.

TOPIC: PROTEIN AND MUSCLE

Protein provides amino acids that support muscle repair
and muscle protein synthesis.

Common dietary protein sources include:
- Eggs.
- Fish.
- Chicken.
- Milk and dairy products.
- Soybeans and legumes.

Overall dietary intake, training stimulus and recovery
all contribute to muscle development.
